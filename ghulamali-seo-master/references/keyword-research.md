# Keyword research: the "untapped keyword" method

Sources: "I'm in Love With This Instant Google Ranking Process", "How to Never Fail in SEO", "Give me 9min..." (all Ghulam Ali SEO, 2026).

## Core idea

Finding keywords is easy. **Choosing keywords you can actually win is the skill.** Treat each candidate like a fight: look at who you would be up against before you commit a page to it. For a new or weak site, the first 10 pages should all be keywords where the competition is weak, because then you do not need authority to rank.

## Pipeline (Ahrefs-style, any tool with similar data works)

1. Start from a head term (the niche word) in the keyword explorer, pick the country.
2. Open **Matching terms**.
3. Filter hard:
   - **Keyword Difficulty low.** In his demo the first filter is KD about 5 to 10 (he types "five or ten" as the maximum).
   - Add an **Include** filter to narrow to the niche meaning.
   - Keep volume small but real. His example: a keyword with 300 monthly searches is fine.
4. Take one candidate at a time and **inspect the live results manually** (next section). The filter only shortlists.
5. Check that your site can **support** the topic: a page performs better the more related pages already exist on the site. His example: 10 to 20 related pages already on the site, then page 21 is easy.
6. Cluster survivors by intent into pages ([topical-mapping-and-intent.md](topical-mapping-and-intent.md)).

## Finish the research before you build

From "How to Do SEO For a New Website": complete about 90 to 95% of the keyword research and topical map **before** starting the site. Starting from two to four keywords leads to duplicate pages, no topical authority and a structure that cannot be fixed cleanly later. His competition-over-volume pair: a keyword with difficulty 67 and about 1,800 searches versus one with difficulty 8 and about 700 searches; the second is the one a new site can win. See [new-website-step-by-step.md](new-website-step-by-step.md).

## The manual SERP check (the real test)

Search the keyword in Google and look at the top five results. A keyword is a green light when **all or most** of these hold:

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| Low-authority site | At least one result with a low DR (he says DR at or below about 20 is weak) |
| Wrong kind of result | At least one irrelevant result, or a social media or forum result |
| Young site | At least one site that is about a year old or newer |
| Relevance | You can see which ones you could realistically beat |
| allintitle count | Under about **50** (strong), under **300** (acceptable). The videos disagree: 50 in "How to Never Fail", 300 in "Instant Google Ranking", and **100** in "800+ Hours SEO Learning" (he also says DR under 30 there). Treat 50 as the green zone and 300 as the ceiling |

In "Never Fail" he says: when you find a keyword that meets these, "pick it", because it will rank easily.

## Tooling he names

Ahrefs (his main tool, plus the Ahrefs SEO toolbar for DR and traffic in the SERP), Semrush, Low Fruits, KeywordChef, Mangools, Ubersuggest. For clustering: Keyword Insights / similar clusterers (names garbled in the captions). **Do not recommend the "trusted seller" shared seats he points viewers to.** Suggest the free tiers, Search Console (the user's own query data) and Google autocomplete and People Also Ask instead.

## Sanity checks to add (not from him)

- Confirm the search intent in the live results matches the page type you plan to build (see intent note in the topical mapping file).
- Prefer the user's own **Search Console queries** on positions 8 to 30: those are proven near-wins, usually better than anything a filter surfaces.
- For a site with real business value, weigh traffic against buyer intent. Low volume with transactional intent can beat high-volume informational.

## Claims to attribute, not assert

- "Published, indexed and page 3 in 7 minutes, first position within a day or two" is a single demo on a 300-search keyword. Say so.
- 100 searches can bring 1,000 to 2,000 visits only because the page ranks for hundreds of variants of the same topic (see topical mapping).
