# Ranking without backlinks: his four steps plus a bonus

Sources: "How to Rank on Google Without Backlinks?" (7K views, about 10 minutes) and the opening and keyword sections of "My SEO Strategy to Rank a website Fast ( SAAS, eCommerce, Business )" (7K). Read from YouTube's transcript panel in the original language and translated by hand.

## Read this first: his position on links

He is **not against backlinks**. Both videos open with proof that sites rank with few or only "foundational" links (stats on screen), then say: build some anyway. Backlinks make a site stronger, keep a ranking stable and protect it, because a competitor will eventually build good links and outrank you. Ranking without them is how you get **in**; some links are how you **stay**. See [backlinks-and-offpage.md](backlinks-and-offpage.md) and [new-website-step-by-step.md](new-website-step-by-step.md) for the link phases.

He adds that this approach does **not work for every site**, because every industry differs. Do not promise the user the same result.

## Step 1: "ingenious" keyword research (worked example: house cleaning in Dubai)

Scenario: a cleaning company in Dubai that sells house cleaning wants house-cleaning business from Google.

**Method A, filter the keyword database (Ahrefs Keywords Explorer):**

1. Enter the core service words and the country, search, open **Matching terms** (about 279,000 keywords in his demo).
2. **Include, any word:** "house" (their service).
3. **Lowest DR filter** (in his demo the filter is set so only keywords where the top five contain a site of that DR or lower appear; he sets it around 20 here, and 5 to 10 in other videos).
4. Result: a short list (about 25 keywords in the demo) such as "house cleaning services Dubai". Repeat for Sharjah, Abu Dhabi and so on if the business serves them.
5. Remove the "house" filter to see other services (window cleaning tips and so on). Informational ones go on the sheet as **blog topics**; commercial ones become service pages. Blogs build awareness; service pages bring buyers. Across the variations he ends with 1,100+ candidate keywords and saves related ones one by one into a sheet.

For other SaaS, e-commerce or business sites he adds these filters: **word count minimum four**, **intent: non-branded**, include your niche word (his example: "barbecue"); then check a random keyword in Google to confirm the filter is not lying.

**Method B, reverse-engineer low-DR competitors:**

1. Search "cleaning services Dubai" and note competitors with **very low DR but good traffic**.
2. Open each in Ahrefs and see which keywords bring the traffic (steam cleaning, villa deep cleaning, floor deep cleaning, ...). These are opportunities; cross-check against what the client actually offers.
3. Test each keyword manually: does any of the top five have DR under 20? In his demo the top results had DR 17, 3, 27, 46, 36, a Reddit result (a weak sign for a service keyword, since a forum thread should not rank for it), 77, 71 and a **DR 0 site on page one**. A DR 0 and a DR 3 site ranking number one and two on a keyword mean it is easy.
4. **Build a list of 50 to 100 competitors** to research further. His claim: "you can do the entire research from your competitors alone."

A few hours of this produces a research sheet. It is not final, but it is the start.

## Step 2: topical mapping (simple)

Group keywords that share intent into one topic, and cover the topic with one page. (His example highlighted two keywords on the sheet that become one page.) Repeat for every keyword on the sheet; the result is the list of pages to build. See [topical-mapping-and-intent.md](topical-mapping-and-intent.md).

## Step 3: content (second most important ranking factor, in his view)

The only goal: the highest-quality piece on that topic. Hand-written or AI is irrelevant to Google, "what matters is how good it is." Four phases: research, structure, write, optimise.

- Research: open the top five, note topics covered, what is missing, average length, whether anyone takes a unique angle.
- Structure: outline, AI-assisted.
- Write: AI handles scaffolding and sentence flow; you add experience, opinion, original data, case studies, real numbers, expert quotes. That is what separates you from "AI slop".
- Optimise: coverage, semantics and depth, with Surfer, Rankability, NeuronWriter or AI.

In the SaaS/e-commerce video he states plainly that AI content can get a page ranked but **cannot keep it there** if it does not serve the visitor: Google tracks what visitors do and drops pages that fail them. See [on-page-and-semantic-content.md](on-page-and-semantic-content.md) for his AI-use checklist.

## Step 4: on-page, with the best of the best

For pages that must rank without links, the on-page work must be thorough: title (keyword first, under 60 characters, clickable); one H1 with the keyword; an H2 with the keyword; H3 for sections; a short descriptive URL with the keyword; a 150-character meta description written like ad copy; **internal links** ("if you skip this all your effort is wasted"); image file names, alt text, WebP.

## Bonus: technical SEO, "a virus that spreads slowly"

Everything is fine at launch; mistakes made without noticing slowly infect the whole site. Examples: pages created and deleted become 404 URLs and waste crawl budget; heavy pages from JavaScript or images of 1, 2 or even 5 MB cause Core Web Vitals problems. From day one: fast hosting (SiteGround or Cloudflare), a lightweight theme (he names GeneratePress), SSL, no heavy designs, a sitemap in Search Console, mobile testing ("60% of your traffic will be mobile", his figure), and no duplicate content (the biggest e-commerce issue). See [technical-seo-audit.md](technical-seo-audit.md).

Course sales, discount codes, workshop pitches, the shared-tool seller.
