# Google Business Profile (local pack) checklist

Sources: "I Ranked a Brand-New GMB in 14 Days. Here's the Exact Playbook." (`tekbSenuX4w`, 12 Jun, 5.1K views) and "Local SEO Case Study How a Business Hit $20K/Month" (`z7vZJP3rYCw`, 10 May, 5K), both read from YouTube's transcript panel in the original Hindi and translated by hand. The second adds the suspension traps, review and engagement signals and the "why does my competitor outrank me" answers (last two sections). **The "14 days" in the title is not shown in the captions**: the spoken content is a standard profile-optimisation checklist, with no day-by-day timeline and no proof for the 14-day claim. Use it as a checklist, not as a promise. His one result claim: a client gets about $10,000 a month of business purely from ranking its Google Business Profile (his claim, not verified).

## Why it matters

Search the service you sell. If your business is not in the **local pack** (the map and three-listing block), you are mostly out of the running for that search. Only businesses with a set-up, well-optimised Google Business Profile (GBP, he says GMB, its old name) get in. Ranking follows Google's three local factors:

| Factor | What it means | What you control |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | How well the business matches the search | primary and secondary categories, the wording you use to describe the business |
| **Distance** | How far the searcher is from the business | nothing (the address), except by making sure the address is correct |
| **Prominence** | How well known and trusted the business looks | number and quality of reviews, citations, links, brand mentions |

## The checklist (his seven techniques)

1. **Choose accurate categories.** The primary category is one of the strongest signals of what you are relevant for. Pick the **most specific** one (an "SEO consultant", not just a broad "SEO" category) and add up to **nine secondary categories**. Change them from the profile's Edit profile screen, business category, pencil icon.
2. **A keyword-aware business description**, written naturally. Research local terms (a seed keyword in a keyword tool, then the variations), prioritise those that match your services, products and location, and **do not stuff keywords**. Write a concise, clear statement of what the business is, who it is for and what makes it different. The limit is **750 characters**, but only the start is shown in results (he says about 100 characters; the visible length varies, so put the key facts first).
3. **High-quality photos and videos.** Follow Google's file-size and format guidance; add variety (staff, customer interactions, the exterior, signature items such as a dish or a mural); add new ones regularly, which makes the profile look active. Customers' uploads also appear on the profile.
4. **Add products and services.** Dashboard, Edit services (pick a category, choose Google's suggested services or write your own); Edit products (name, price, description, publish). This helps the profile appear in more relevant searches and helps a customer judge fit.
5. **Get reviews, steadily.** The count of positive reviews feeds prominence. His claim: a business getting **at least 5 new reviews a month** can outperform a competitor with 50 reviews over two years but only 2 in the last six months. How to ask: at **natural moments** right after a purchase or a good interaction; keep the request simple, with the **shareable review link** from the GBP dashboard (for example in a post-purchase email). **Follow Google's policies**: no incentives, no paying for reviews, no fake reviews, no pressure. **Reply to every review**, including negative ones.
6. **Post regularly.** Three types: updates (news, tips, announcements), offers (specials, discounts, limited deals) and events (with dates). Always include a call-to-action button. Regular posts show the business is active.
7. **Keep information accurate and consistent.** Review the profile now and then and make sure hours, address, phone and website match your **local citations** (directory listings). Google cross-references your details to check legitimacy.

## Do not get suspended: the business name (`z7vZJP3rYCw`)

His case: a site doing about $20,000 a month from local SEO plus about 150 calls a month from its profile, no ads (his claim; he says 70 to 80% of what his agency does is the same from client to client). His first point is the one that can undo everything: **a wrong business name gets the profile suspended**, and reinstating it is painful.

- Use only the **legal business name**, the one on your documents and your shop sign.
- No keywords in the name ("Best Dental Clinic in Lahore Khan" for "Khan Dental Clinic").
- No location in the name ("Khan Dental Clinic DHA Phase 5 Lahore").
- No services in the name ("Khan Dental Clinic Teeth Whitening Implants Braces").
- If the name is already stuffed, edit it back now.

**First decide where you need to rank.** A hyper-local business with a small service area (a salon: nobody travels 10 to 20 km) is better off winning the **map pack** than the ordinary results. A restaurant may draw people 5 to 10 km for taste or delivery, so it needs both the map pack and normal results.

## How reviews and engagement really work

- **No public "review quality score".** He calls the idea a myth: Google has never confirmed a per-review quality number that counts directly in rankings. But Google **does** evaluate review patterns. Red flags: 30 reviews in a week after 4 in six months; suspicious reviewer behaviour (new account that only ever reviewed you, a location that makes no sense, no history); sudden unnatural spikes, for example 50 reviews in a day from an incentive. A review by an established local guide with 200 reviews likely carries more weight than one from a brand-new account.
- **Authentic, detailed and naturally paced beats volume.** "20 real detailed reviews beat 200 fake ones." One review like "the doctor gave me 30 minutes, the root canal was painless, friendly staff" is worth more than a pile of generic five-stars.
- **Engagement signals** (his list): clicks to call, direction requests, website clicks, photo views, how often the hours and the services are checked. They tell Google the listing is useful, "your digital shop window". Improve them with: recent real photos (not stock) of interior, exterior, team, products and services; frequent posts (he says daily; weekly is more realistic for most); **accurate opening hours** (wrong hours "silently kill" the listing); Q&A or FAQ content you write yourself where the profile still offers it (Google has been changing that feature, so check); and **landing pages on your website that match the service and area** (generic pages raise bounce rate).
- He admits **nobody knows each signal's exact weight**; the combined picture is what counts.

## Why a competitor outranks you

A common client question: "my competitor is 2 km away and has fewer reviews, yet ranks above me." His answers: **proximity to the searcher** (it depends where the searcher stands at that moment; you cannot fix it); **category relevance** (a dentist who chose "medical clinic" as the primary category loses dental searches); **brand strength and prominence** (news mentions, citations, directories, social presence, backlinks; a competitor present for 5 years has a natural gap on you); **review pattern and trust** (80 reviews in three months can score lower than 40 spread over two years); **engagement ratio** (a higher call, click and direction rate reads as more useful).

## How to use it

- **Local service business, or a shop with a physical address:** do this before any content work; it is the cheapest local visibility. Tie it to [local-seo-keywords.md](local-seo-keywords.md) (neighbourhood, "near me", landmark and competitor-name keywords all use the same profile) and to the AI-visibility audit in [geo-aeo-playbook.md](geo-aeo-playbook.md), where the identical name, address and phone everywhere is also what language models trust.
- **An online-only shop** has no local pack to win unless it has a real address; skip this and use [shopify-seo.md](shopify-seo.md).
- **Do not mix profiles:** one verified profile per real location, no fake addresses; that breaks Google's rules and risks suspension.
