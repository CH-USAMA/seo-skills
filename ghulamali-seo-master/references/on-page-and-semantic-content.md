# On-page SEO and semantic content

Source: "Give me 9min...", "How to Never Fail in SEO", "Instant Google Ranking Process", "Stop Chasing Backlinks", "30-Day SEO+GEO Roadmap".

## The on-page list

Title tag, meta description, URL, content quality, heading tags, image optimisation (alt text), internal links, page speed, mobile friendliness, schema. He calls on-page "your main weapon" for ranking pages quickly, and **content quality is the top item**.

### Where the target keyword should appear

Meta title, meta description, H1, at least one H2, the first paragraph, and image alt text. Then also: link internally to other pages, give at least one external link, add a video, add schema.

(Do not stuff it. Semantic writing, below, matters more than repetition.)

## Semantic content writing (his favourite topic)

Old way: repeat the keyword a set number of times. New way: **write about the whole topic as an expert would**, and use the full set of related terms and entities.

Process:

1. Search the keyword in Google. Open the top 3 to 4 competitors and scan what they cover. That gives the outline.
2. Run the keyword through a content optimiser (Surfer, NeuronWriter, Phrase, Rankability named). It returns **a list of semantic words and phrases with suggested usage counts**.
3. Export that list and give it to the writer, human or AI, with the outline. Use the terms naturally across the article.
4. Cover the topic from every angle, add entities (people, things, concepts), define their relationships, and talk about related subtopics so topical authority builds.
5. Add **experience-based information**. Not generic text.
6. Build a **content cluster**: not one article but several articles around the topic, linked together.
7. Apply schema and an E-E-A-T pass (author, dates, sources).

Benefits he lists: higher rankings, featured snippets, voice-search readiness, lower bounce rate, future-proofing.

## When AI writing is acceptable (his checklist)

He says "95% of people use AI" and "no one hires writers now", but he only recommends AI output if **all** of these are true:

- Semantically optimised with the term list
- Contains experience-based information
- Better than what the top competitors have
- Easy to read and skimmable
- In native-quality language with no grammar or punctuation mistakes
- In active voice

Otherwise hire a writer. A writer in Pakistan costs about Rs 5,000 to 10,000 per article; an AI subscription gives dozens of drafts for about $20. If you cannot judge quality, he says it is better to pay a strong writer for the first 5 to 10 pages so that the ranking test is fair.

Tools named for AI content: Claude (for creation), plus a term list from Surfer, NeuronWriter, Phrase or Rankability.

## Which pages are written for whom

| Page type | Write for |
|---|---|
| Blog, product, service pages | The user **and** Google |
| Category / listing pages | Google mostly (users do not read them) |

## Readability and formatting

Short paragraphs, skimmable, easy to digest, clear headings, and media (image or video) every three paragraphs or so to break the page.

## Internal linking (his "instant ranking" lever)

Internal links behave like backlinks. When pages that already have authority link to a new page, authority transfers, and Google trusts the new page by default, which shortens ranking time. If the new page is the only page on the site, it has no topical authority to borrow, so it needs backlink signals instead. See [backlinks-and-offpage.md](backlinks-and-offpage.md).

Practical rule: when you publish page 21 on a topic that already has 10 to 20 related pages, add links to it **from** those pages immediately.

## Answer-first (shared with GEO)

Put a short direct answer in the first sentence or two, then expand. Both Google and AI retrieval reward it. Full detail in [geo-aeo-playbook.md](geo-aeo-playbook.md).

## Quick quality gate before publishing

- [ ] One primary keyword, intent matches the page type
- [ ] Outline covers what the top pages cover plus one useful extra
- [ ] Semantic terms used naturally
- [ ] Keyword in title, description, H1, an H2, first paragraph, an image alt
- [ ] Linked to from existing relevant pages, and links out to relevant pages
- [ ] At least about 500 words for a service page (300 is the absolute floor)
- [ ] Schema added and validated
- [ ] A human has read it for accuracy and tone
