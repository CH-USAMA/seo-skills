# SEO for a new website, step by step (his full order of work)

Source: "How to Do SEO For a New Website ( Step by Step )" (28K views, recorded 13 December). The 30-day plan in [new-site-30-day-roadmap.md](new-site-30-day-roadmap.md) is the week-by-week version with the GEO layer; this file is the older, simpler end-to-end sequence and carries the reasoning behind each step.

## The framing he opens with

- It took him two years to rank his first site; after 500+ sites in 10 years he says he can rank one within a month, and has ranked some keywords within a week (his example: a page posted on 8 December that sat in first position by 13 December). These are his claims about his own sites. Quote them as "his case studies", never as what the user should expect.
- **The 2016 approach** he now rejects: pick keywords with volume in the thousands or lakhs, start a site after half-baked research, build it only to rank, with no value for the people who arrive.
- **The current approach**: every step is strategic. The order is research, topical map, semantic content, on-page, technical, backlinks.

## Step 1: research (finish it before you build)

Three rules:

1. **Choose by competition, not by volume.** His pair of examples: "insurance Dubai" has difficulty 67 and about 1,800 searches; "travel insurance for [a visa type]" has difficulty 8 and about 700 searches. The second is the keyword a new site can win. He says this approach doubled a client's traffic in two months (he has a separate case-study video; not transcribed here).
2. **Complete about 90 to 95% of the research before launching.** Starting a site from two to four keywords causes: no topical map, so no topical authority; repeated keywords, so duplicate pages; a structure that cannot be made proper later; pages left incomplete. This is the part of his advice most worth applying to any new project: do the full keyword and cluster list first, then build.
3. **Do the topical mapping while researching**, so you rank for a topic and not a keyword. His example: a page targeted at a keyword with 100 searches that brings about 2,000 visits, because the one page ranks for about 1,300 near-identical keywords at different positions. Group the similar keywords under one topic, make one page for that topic. See [topical-mapping-and-intent.md](topical-mapping-and-intent.md) and [keyword-research.md](keyword-research.md).

## Step 2: semantic content

Write on the topic, not on the keyword. Do not repeat a keyword 10, 20 or 50 times. His proof: a page ranking third for "windows gaming laptop" where that exact phrase does not appear in the content.

Process: search the keyword, scan three or four of the top pages to get the outline, then get a semantic term list from a content tool (Rankability, Surfer, NeuronWriter; he demonstrates Rankability's Content Optimizer, which returns terms with suggested usage counts), export it, and give outline plus list to the writer.

On cost: he says content is Google's second most important ranking factor and that well-written content "costs once and pays again and again". Human writer preferred when the budget allows (he quotes Rs 5,000 to 10,000 per article, local pricing). AI is acceptable only if the output is semantically written, skimmable, better than the top competitors, easy to read, in the reader's native language, free of grammar and punctuation errors, and in active voice. Details in [on-page-and-semantic-content.md](on-page-and-semantic-content.md).

## Step 3: on-page SEO

A checklist done on each page. For a content page:

- keyword in the **title** and in a **heading**
- keyword in the **alt text** of an image
- at least **one video** on the page
- an **external link** and an **internal link**

He audits any page with the Chrome extension he calls "Detailed" (the Detailed SEO extension): it shows title, meta description, URL, canonical, headings, image alt text and schema in one panel.

The most important on-page item is the **internal linking structure**: it connects the site and spreads the authority that backlinks bring to every page. A page that no other page links to is an **orphan** and, in his words, will never rank. See [technical-seo-audit.md](technical-seo-audit.md) for how to find orphans.

## Step 4: technical SEO

The short list he gives for a new site:

1. **Fast hosting** (he names SiteGround) so Google can crawl in milliseconds and visitors get a page in under 3 seconds. Slow pages show up as Core Web Vitals issues in Search Console.
2. **SSL** (HTTPS).
3. **Mobile responsive** on desktop, tablet and phone.
4. **XML sitemap** submitted in Search Console, so the site is indexed sooner.
5. Then the long list: keep **redirects** to a minimum; fix **404s** and remove deleted pages from Google; check **robots.txt** (the first file Google reads); avoid **cannibalisation** (two pages targeting one keyword); avoid **duplicate content**, using canonicals where the same content must exist twice.

## Step 5: backlinks, in two phases

His position: he is "not a fan" of backlinks because they are the most expensive part of SEO, and his agency never makes them the main factor in a plan; the plan is to rank without them first. But he is explicit that they matter: they **keep rankings stable** and stop a competitor with good links from overtaking you.

| When | What to build |
|---|---|
| Months 1 and 2 | **Foundational links**: social media profiles, local citations, Web 2.0, forum links, profile links. Cheap, low risk, proves the site exists. |
| From month 3 | **Quality links**: guest posts, niche edits, link exchanges, podcast appearances. Apply the quality tests in [backlinks-and-offpage.md](backlinks-and-offpage.md). |

**If a competitor outranks you despite those links:** put the competitor into a backlink tool, open its backlinks report, sort by **new** links (the recently gained ones), keep one link per referring domain, then sort by authority. These are the sites that recently chose to link to your competitor, so they are the ones to approach for a link of your own.
