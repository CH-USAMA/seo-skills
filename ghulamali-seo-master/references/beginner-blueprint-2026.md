# The beginner blueprint: intent, the keyword sweet spot, money pages, a 30-day plan

Source: "Master SEO in 2026: Google + AI Search" (7K views, about 12:38). Read from YouTube's transcript panel in the original language and translated by hand. It is his beginner layer; the advanced layer (crawling, indexing, algorithms, advanced technical and link building) he keeps for his paid course.

## Framing claims (attribute to him)

- "SEO is not dead": Google handles about 14 billion searches a day, up about 20% last year, and sends about 190 times more traffic to websites than ChatGPT does. These are his figures; they are not checked here.
- ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini run on **Bing and Google indexes**. If you do not exist in Google, you do not exist for AI. SEO is the base AI search stands on, not a competitor to it.
- The order for a beginner: search intent, keyword research, content writing, on-page, off-page, AI rankings.

## Search intent: four types

| Type | Example | Visitor | Modifiers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | how to train a puppy | Wants to learn, will not buy now | starts with how to, what, if, can you, is it |
| Commercial | best dog training class | Comparing, ready to spend | best + product, product + reviews, cheap + product |
| Transactional | dog training class near me sign up | The exact buyer | services + local area, buy + product, product name |
| Navigational | a brand name + the thing | Wants one brand; effectively buying intent | brand name |

**Finding intent:** search your keyword and read the top 10. Blog posts mean informational, comparison articles mean commercial, product pages mean transactional. **A short keyword means more competition and mixed intent** (his example: "coffee", about 600K searches, mixed results). A long-tail query ("best dark roast coffee beans for espresso": a few hundred searches, all comparison guides) has clear intent and less competition, which is the best choice for beginners.

## The keyword "sweet spot": four checks

1. **Demand:** do people search for it? Ranking for a term nobody searches pays nothing.
2. **Market fit:** does it match the business?
3. **Intent:** what does the searcher want, and do you actually provide it?
4. **Difficulty:** can you realistically rank? A new site cannot beat Amazon for "running shoes", but can for "best running shoes for flat feet in winter".

## Research steps (Ahrefs, any tool with similar data works)

1. Start with a **topic** (the client's business, the shop, your portfolio). Keywords come after topics.
2. Keywords Explorer: seed word, country, search, then **Matching terms**.
3. Apply the low-competition filters (see [keyword-research.md](keyword-research.md)).
4. Pick one, search it in Google, and look for a site with **low DR and high traffic**. Open that site in the tool: its pages show what is already working, "done for you" research.
5. Where the top five show low-DR sites or irrelevant results, competition is low.
6. **Group repeated keywords** under one topic: topical mapping. His example: "pour over coffee ratio", "cold brew ratio" and "coffee brewing methods" are one cluster, internally linked, which becomes topical authority.

## Money pages first, then clusters one at a time

- **Money pages** convert directly: product, service, pricing and feature pages. Build them first, targeting transactional and commercial keywords (his examples: buy [product] online; cyber security assessment services; proposal software for agencies).
- Then take **one cluster** and make it a funnel: money pages at the bottom (for example a coffee subscription), commercial pieces above ("best coffee subscription", "subscription box comparison"), educational content above that (brew ratios, grind size). Each upper layer links to the next and down to the money pages.
- **Finish one cluster before starting the next.** Google does not judge a page in isolation: covering a whole journey (what is ransomware, how to prevent it, a response checklist) earns trust.

## Content: "5x better", four phases

1. **Research:** read the top five results and note what topics they cover, what they miss, the average length, and whether anyone takes a unique angle. Do this by hand for the first two or three articles.
2. **Structure:** build the outline (AI can draft it, then refine).
3. **Write:** AI is fine for scaffolding, basic definitions and sentence flow, but add what AI cannot: your **experience**, your **opinion** (overrated, underrated, wrong), **original data**, **case studies**, and specific examples with real numbers, expert quotes, podcasts or books. This is his "SEO moat" and what separates the page from AI slop.
4. **Optimise:** check coverage, semantics and depth with Surfer, Rankability or NeuronWriter; since those are costly he says AI can do the check too.

## On-page checklist

- Title tag: keyword, under 60 characters, clickable.
- One H1 (the page title, with the keyword). One H2 containing the keyword as the main section; other sections as H3. (Treat the strict "one H1 / one H2" rule as his house style; Google does not require it.)
- URL short, descriptive, with the keyword.
- Meta description 150 to 160 characters, written like ad copy; it does not help ranking but raises CTR.
- **Internal links:** link money pages to related content. "The most important; do not skip."
- Images: descriptive file names, keyword in alt text, WebP format.

## Backlinks (the hardest part)

- **Foundational links:** Web 2.0, forums, profile links, video links.
- **Quality links, three ways to start:**
  1. **Build a source:** publish original data, original analysis or free tools that journalists cite.
  2. **Source of sources:** free platforms where journalists post daily queries; reply with quotes, around 15 minutes a day, about 100 queries; "a numbers game, but the most accessible tactic for beginners".
  3. **Linkable assets:** free calculators, comprehensive guides, templates (non-coders can now build simple tools in about 10 minutes with AI).
- Beginner prerequisite: first produce 10 to 15 great pieces of content, then start the source-of-sources outreach.

## AI search visibility

Basic SEO is the same for AI search: comprehensive content, clear structure, topic clusters, authoritative citations. Two AI-specific tactics: get placed in **"best of" roundup articles** (AI cites them heavily; do outreach), and **track where you are mentioned** in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity (he mentions Surfer's tracking). Overlaps with [geo-aeo-playbook.md](geo-aeo-playbook.md).

## His 30-day plan

| Week | Do |
|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | Set up Google Search Console (free). Find 10 to 15 keywords. Check intent for the top five using the sweet-spot framework. Identify your money pages. |
| 2. Money pages | Build or optimise the most important money pages: research, structure, write, optimise, apply the on-page checklist. |
| 3. Build the funnel | Pick one cluster. Publish one mid-funnel or top-funnel piece and link it to the money pages. Do not ignore internal links. |
| 4. Promote and monitor | Spend 15 to 25 minutes a day on source-of-sources outreach. Check Search Console: rising impressions and a position moving from page 5 toward page 3 are early signs it works. |

His caution: do not expect extraordinary growth in 30 days; expect good signals. Compare with [new-site-30-day-roadmap.md](new-site-30-day-roadmap.md), which adds the GEO layer and the weekly measuring loop.
