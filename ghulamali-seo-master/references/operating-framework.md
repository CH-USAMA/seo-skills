# Operating framework: consistency, not tricks

Source: "How to Never Fail in SEO" (11K), "How I Built an Online Business - After Testing 6 Skills" (11K), "Give me 9min...".

## "A project that gets time intentionally and holistically will never fail"

He shows three sites: a student's, an agency client's, and one of his own that he created and abandoned (it failed). "Giving attention" does not mean staring at the site; it means **consistently doing SEO activities, monitoring, updating and upgrading until the target result is reached.**

The five buckets he lists (and their reference files):

1. **Keyword research**: low-hanging fruit first. [keyword-research.md](keyword-research.md)
2. **Website structure**: URL hierarchy, parent topics and subtopics, pillar pages, keyword mapping and clustering. [topical-mapping-and-intent.md](topical-mapping-and-intent.md)
3. **On-page and content**: semantic writing, formatting, keyword placement, internal links, schema. [on-page-and-semantic-content.md](on-page-and-semantic-content.md)
4. **Technical SEO**: "reducing the distance between your site and Google"; speed under 2 seconds, SSL, mobile, redirects, orphans, cannibalisation, canonicals, schema. [technical-seo-audit.md](technical-seo-audit.md)
5. **Off-page**: more than backlinks (branded searches, engagement, social), but quality links first. [backlinks-and-offpage.md](backlinks-and-offpage.md)

Plus, from the 9-minute video, the sequence "research, on-page, technical, off-page" is the broad checklist for confirming that your SEO is correct.

## Patience numbers he gives

- SEO usually needs **6 to 12 months** for good results, "8 to 10 months" is typical, **if** implemented correctly. More mistakes means a longer wait.
- A client may get nervous in 2 to 3 months; real results take 6 to 7.
- You do not need a course to start; you need enough SEO knowledge to know the work is right, and patience. A mentor shortens the learning curve.
- He says his sites have not been hit by updates: "I give value to just one user", working within Google's guidelines.

## His five-factor scorecard for choosing an online skill or business

Rate each option on: **upfront capital, scalability, fulfilment difficulty, risk, learning difficulty.**

| Option | Capital | Scalable | Fulfilment | Risk | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dropshipping | High | Needs money | Hard (supplier) | High | Easy to learn |
| E-commerce SEO | High | Hard | Hard unless you manufacture | High | High |
| Facebook / Google ads | Low to start | Hard (needs budget) | Hard (results promised) | High | High (platform changes) |
| Video editing | High (PC) | Needs people | Hard (revisions) | Low | Low |
| SEO | Very low ($40 to 50 domain and hosting + ~$20/month tool) | Easy | Medium (clients impatient) | High (Google updates) | Easy to start, needs patience |

His conclusion: video editing and SEO score best. For getting customers he lists four routes: door to door, paid ads, content creation (about a year to traction), and SEO (build a site, target keywords, wait). SEO can be done part time.

Use this scorecard when the user asks which new idea, site or service to pursue.

## Diversify income

His own story (waiter, AdSense site hit by a Google update, content agency killed by ChatGPT, Fiverr, then multiple income sources) leads to one rule: **do not rely on one income source**. Content creation, own sites and client work together.

## Using this for the user's decisions

- When a user asks "what should I do next", rank options by this scorecard plus evidence from the site.
- Do not use his income or student-result claims in client-facing material. They are marketing for his course.
