# Backlinks and off-page SEO

Source: "Instant Google Ranking Process", "Give me 9min...", "The Only Video You Need to Watch", "How to Never Fail in SEO", "Stop Chasing Backlinks".

## What counts as a quality link

All of these together:

- From a **topically relevant** website
- Inside the **body content** of a page (not header, footer, sidebar or comments)
- From a site with real authority (he uses DR 70+ as a strong signal, DR 37 as the best link of a small competitor)
- On a page that **gets traffic from Google itself** and ranks for real keywords
- The linking page is **actually indexed**. If it is not indexed, Google never counts the link.
- Dofollow

His diagnosis of cheap link packages: a "DR 70 to 80 guest post for under Rs 1,000 or $10" is normally a site built only to sell guest posts, ranking for adult or irrelevant topics, with manipulated DR and traffic, and the page that holds your link never gets indexed. Such links give no value.

## Two ways to get an external link (and how to pick)

| Method | What you get | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Guest post | A new page that links to you | The page is new, nothing else links to it, so Google counts it only after it is indexed and it takes time to build authority. Not instant. |
| Niche edit (link insertion) | A link added to an **existing** page | Check the page already has backlinks, already gets Google traffic, has internal links from the same site, and the site has authority. Faster and stronger. |

Other methods he lists: broken-link building, resource-page links, Web 2.0, forum and profile links, digital PR, original data as a link magnet. For a free backlink and posting list he points to his site; BeyondSEO ships a 206-entry free and paid source library that serves the same purpose.

## The "do not chase links" strategy

His headline: you can get large Google traffic **without** chasing backlinks when you pick low-competition keywords, map topics properly and write semantic content (see [keyword-research.md](keyword-research.md) and [topical-mapping-and-intent.md](topical-mapping-and-intent.md)). One of his clients' traffic "came from low competition keywords and we spent nothing on backlinks".

Backlinks matter when the SERP is strong. Google itself says PageRank is part of its core ranking systems, and he agrees: quality over quantity.

## Internal links as the first link source

Existing pages carry authority. A link from them to a new page transfers some of it, which is why he says a new page on a site with 10 to 20 related pages ranks fast. A brand-new site with a single page has nothing to transfer, so that page needs backlink signals instead.

## Timing: foundational first, quality from month 3

For a new site he builds **foundational links** in months 1 and 2 (social profiles, local citations, Web 2.0, forum and profile links) and **quality links** from month 3 (guest posts, niche edits, exchanges, podcasts). Links keep rankings stable and stop a competitor overtaking you; they are not the main ranking plan. If a competitor outranks you, open its backlinks report in a backlink tool, sort by newest links, one per referring domain, then by authority, and approach those sites. Full context in [new-website-step-by-step.md](new-website-step-by-step.md).

## Link quality quick tests (use before buying or accepting)

1. Open the proposed linking page. Is it indexed? (`site:` the exact URL, or Search Console if you own it)
2. Does the site rank for real keywords and have organic traffic that is not manipulated?
3. Is the topic related to yours? Does it host adult, gambling or unrelated guest content?
4. Will the link sit in the body, dofollow, with a normal sentence around it?
5. Is DR consistent with the number of referring domains? Thousands of referring domains with a low DR means garbage links.

## Things not to copy from the videos

- "Making anchor text dirty is not an issue": his casual remark. Natural, varied anchors are the safe approach; see BeyondSEO's `anchor-text-strategy.md`.
- Buying links from the seller promoted in the videos, or any bulk guest-post vendor.
- Cheap shared Ahrefs seats. Use legitimate access.
