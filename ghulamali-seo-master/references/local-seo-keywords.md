# Local SEO keyword research: the five hidden patterns

Source: "I've never shared before these Local SEO Keyword Research Methods" (7K views, about 4 minutes). Read from YouTube's transcript panel in the original language and translated by hand.

**His point:** almost every local business targets only the obvious pattern, "service in city". That is not wrong, but it is not enough. Competitors win with keywords that keyword tools **do not show**, and each visitor from them is a likely customer. He says an agency client ranks for city, "near me" and neighbourhood keywords and gets steady business from pure SEO (his claim; shown on screen).

He has audited 500+ sites and says nearly all service sites use only the "service in city" pattern.

## The five methods

| # | Method | Pattern | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Neighbourhood level** | service + neighbourhood, district or **zip code** (his examples: "architect in [town]", "lawyer in [district]", "car repair" plus a zip code) | Searches may be 10 or fewer, but intent is very high, competition is low, conversion is much better. |
| 2 | **"Near me" with urgency modifiers** | "open near me", "open on Sunday", "open today", "opening hours", "24 hours" (his example: beauty salon) | People rarely plan a salon visit 10 days ahead unless it is a wedding; they search and buy the same day. |
| 3 | **Hyper-local landmarks** | service + landmark: "dentist near a mall", "restaurant by the stadium", "lawyer close to a courthouse", gym or coffee shop near a university or park | People search by landmark. Google's suggestion box shows these because people really type them. Tools do not list them, so you need **local knowledge** of malls, universities, hospitals and schools in the area. |
| 4 | **Competitor-name variations** | competitor name + alternatives, vs, "better than", "cheaper than", reviews | Intercepts searchers who are already looking for a different option than your competitor. |
| 5 | **Mobile-specific queries** | "directions to", "walking distance", phone number, "is [business] open now", "closest to me" | Mobile searchers ask different questions. Optimise the site for mobile (Google indexes mobile first). |

**The worked example he gives:** "architect in Lahore" has about 450 searches and high competition, so a new page may sit on page two to four for a long time. "Architect in [one specific district of Lahore]" has about 30 searches and no competition; he expects a page to rank within a month, and the 30 visitors are very likely to become customers.

## How he finds them (no paid tool needed)

- **Google Autocomplete:** type partial queries and read the suggestions.
- **People Also Ask:** mine the questions.
- **Reverse-engineer local Google Business Profiles:** look at what competitors' profiles and categories say.
- **Local subreddits:** see what people in the area ask for.
